T6ECI — Structure & State Machinery 🏢☐ revised
📖 Definition
Election Commission of India (ECI) — an autonomous, permanent constitutional body (established 25 January 1950) vested with the superintendence, direction, and control of the entire process for the conduct of elections. Articles 324 to 329 of the Constitution provide for its establishment and define its powers and duties.
💡 Samajh (Hinglish)
Election ka referee = ECI! Independent boss hai — kisi party ka nahi sunta, sirf Constitution ka. Kab bana? 25 January 1950 (isliye National Voters' Day bhi isi din!). Kaam: Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha, Vidhan Sabha, Vidhan Parishad, President aur Vice President — sabki elections conduct karta hai. State level pe machinery: CEO (Chief Electoral Officer) boss, aur DMs/SDMs/Tahsildars ko election duty pe designations milti hain — DEO, RO, ERO. Poora system — har level pe officers ready!
🌳 System — Election Machinery (kis level pe kaun)
  • ECI (national level) — superintendence, direction and control of the entire election process
  • State level — Chief Electoral Officer (CEO) of the State
    • Appointed by the ECI from among civil servants, in consultation with the State Government
  • District level & below — State Government officers assigned election duties
    • District Magistrates, Sub-Divisional Magistrates, Tahsildars → designated as District Election Officers (DEO), Returning Officers (RO), Electoral Registration Officers (ERO)
✍️ Exam Points
  • For democracy to be realised, elections must be conducted in a free, fair and transparent manner.
  • The Constitution and parliamentary laws define the functions and powers of all stakeholders: the ECI, political parties, civil society, media, and voters.
T7ECI — 4 Main Functions 🛠️☐ revised
💡 Samajh (Hinglish)
ECI ke 4 bade kaam: (1) Electoral roll banana — har ghar me enumerator bhej ke voters ki list; SIR (Special Intensive Revision) se list saaf-suthri — naye 18+ wale add, dead/shifted/duplicate hatao! (2) Schedule decide karna — kab election? Mausam, fasal ka cycle, exams, festivals — sab dekh ke. (3) Parties register karna + symbols dena — national/state/RUPP classification. (4) Free & fair ensure karna — sabko include karo: PwDs, 85+ senior citizens (ghar pe vote!), EVM me Braille. "No Voter to Be Left Behind!"
🌳 System — ECI Functions (detail me)
  • 1. Creates the electoral roll
    • Official enumerators visit every household to collect data on eligible electors
    • Rolls prepared constituency-wise, organised polling station-wise; only those on the roll can vote
    • SIR (Special Intensive Revision) — updating, verifying, correcting rolls: adds young voters who just turned 18; deletes names of voters who died, shifted, enrolled twice, or are untraceable
    • Time given for claims and objections before the final roll is published
  • 2. Decides the schedule and dates
    • Activated when the 5-year term ends or the legislature is dissolved early
    • Considers: weather conditions, agricultural cycle, school/university exams, festivals
  • 3. Registers political parties & allocates symbols
    • Only parties registered with the ECI can contest; independent candidates can also contest
    • Insists on inner-party democracy (periodic organisational elections)
    • Classifies parties: recognised national / state-regional / registered-unrecognised
    • Acts as a quasi-judicial body in disputes over party recognition and symbol allotment
  • 4. Ensures free and fair elections
    • Inclusive initiatives for PwDs, senior citizens, transgender persons, PVTGs
    • Voting from home for senior citizens above 85 and PwDs with 40%+ benchmark disability — extended across India for the first time in the 2024 General Elections
    • EVMs with Braille for visually impaired voters
✍️ Exam Points
  • National Voters' Day — 25 January every year (ECI's foundation day), with a voters' pledge: to vote in every election fearlessly and without being influenced by religion, race, caste, community, language or any inducement.
  • India's electoral exercise is unparalleled — over 96.8 crore eligible voters (2024) across diverse regions and terrains; the ECI uses ICT and e-governance extensively.
T8ECI Digital Ecosystem — Apps 📱☐ revised
💡 Samajh (Hinglish)
ECI ek tech-savvy boss hai — har problem ka ek app! Service voters (army etc.) ke liye ETPBS (electronically ballot ghar bhejo), voters ke liye Helpline App, violation report karne ke liye cVIGIL (flying squads turant pahunchti hain!), forms ke liye ERONET, election vehicles ke liye Sugam, candidates ke liye Suvidha (permissions etc.), aur PwDs ke liye Saksham. International level pe bhi boss: IEVP programme — 28 EMBs + 3 international organisations ke saath MoUs!
🌳 System — Digital Ecosystem (app → kaam)
  • ETPBS — Electronically Transmitted Postal Ballot System for service voters; cast votes from anywhere outside their constituency
  • Voter Helpline App — multipurpose: registration, transposition, verification, election news and updates
  • cVIGIL App — report Model Code of Conduct violations and misconduct in minutes; flying squads navigate to the spot via auto-location
  • ERONET — seamless form processing, dashboards, role-based access, maintenance of e-rolls
  • Sugam — manages vehicles requisitioned from other departments for election work
  • Suvidha — for candidates and parties: nomination forms, permissions (meetings, rallies, vehicles, loudspeakers) with real-time status
  • Saksham App — customised services for PwDs: registration, finding polling stations
✍️ Exam Points
  • Motto: "No Voter to Be Left Behind".
  • IEVP (International Election Visitors' Programme) — flagship ECI programme for cooperation with Election Management Bodies (EMBs) of other countries; MoUs signed with 28 EMBs and three international organisations — IFES, International IDEA, and the United Nations.
T9Political Parties + Defection + Recognition 🎭☐ revised
📖 Definitions
Defection — abandoning or switching from the political party under whose banner a candidate was elected; may involve joining another party or acting independently of the party's decisions (e.g., voting in the legislature).  |  Whip — the party's instruction on how to vote in the House.
💡 Samajh (Hinglish)
Parties = bridge between janta aur government. Kaam: voters ko informed choice dena, public opinion organise karna, issues pe campaign, meaningful options dena. Lekin drama bhi hai: defection — ticket jeetne ke baad party chhodna ("party-hopping")! Isliye Anti-Defection Law (1985, 52nd Constitutional Amendment) aayi: party chhodo ya whip ke against vote karo → disqualification; decision Speaker/Chairman ka. Alliances ka bhi pattern: 1967 se single-party era khatam, 1977 me Janata Party (first coalition), phir NDA vs UPA (2023 me UPA → INDIA alliance reconstitute hui).
🌳 System — Role of Political Parties + Recognition Criteria
  • Functions: enable voters to make informed decisions; organise public opinion; campaign on specific issues; offer voters meaningful choices
  • Flow: Citizens → Political Parties (organise opinion, contest elections, plan campaigns, prepare manifestos)
    • Winning party — forms the government; formulates and implements policies; ensures accountability
    • Opposition parties — participate in debates and committees; hold the government accountable
  • Party recognition (by ECI):
    • National party — any one: 6% valid votes in 4+ states + 4 Lok Sabha seats; OR 2% of LS seats from at least 3 states; OR recognised as a state party in 4+ states
    • State party — any one: 6% votes in state Assembly + 2 Assembly seats; OR 6% votes in LS from the state + 1 LS seat; OR 3% of Assembly seats (or 3 seats); OR 1 LS seat for every 25 LS seats allotted to the state; OR 8% of valid votes in the state
    • RUPP (Registered Unrecognised) — parties that have not secured enough votes, or never contested since registration
  • Alliances: 1967 — end of single-party dominance; 1977 — Janata Party (first coalition government, against the Emergency backdrop); since late 1990s — NDA (led by BJP) and UPA (led by INC) — dissolved 2023, reconstituted as the Indian National Developmental Inclusive Alliance
✍️ Exam Points — Anti-Defection Law
  • Passed in 1985 through the 52nd Constitutional Amendment Act; purpose — prevent defection and provide stability to the elected government.
  • If a member of Parliament or a State Assembly voluntarily gives up party membership or votes against the party's whip, they can be disqualified from the House.
  • The Speaker or Chairman of the House decides such cases.
  • India has a multi-party system — many parties exist and compete, reflecting the diversity of languages, cultures, religions and regions.
  • Defection can be seen as political opportunism — or as upholding conscience and responding to changing public expectations.
T10Challenges to Free & Fair Elections ⚠️☐ revised
💡 Samajh (Hinglish)
96.8 crore voters, thousands of polling stations, hundreds of parties — election conduct karna world's biggest event management hai! Challenges: misinformation, fake news, intimidation (dhamki/vote dabav). ECI ke jawab: RP Acts (laws), MCC — Model Code of Conduct (election-time ke rules), EVMs + VVPAT (vote ka paper proof — machine ke saath slip dikhti hai!), aur voters' awareness campaigns. Ishani wale case me dekho — wheelchair, volunteers, VVPAT slip, 89 saal ki dadi ka ghar se vote... system har kisi ko include karta hai!
✍️ Exam Points — Before we move on (book summary)
  • Casting a vote is a constitutional right as well as a duty of every citizen.
  • Elections are the soul of democracy — they give every citizen an equal voice in the formation of a government.
  • Laws like the RP Acts of 1950 and 1951, and bodies like the Delimitation Commission and the ECI, keep the process free, fair and transparent.
  • Political parties present diverse programmes and policies, helping voters make informed choices.
  • ECI measures against challenges: RPA 1950/1951, Model Code of Conduct (MCC), EVMs, VVPAT (Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail), and voters' awareness campaigns.
  • With constant vigilance and active citizen participation, elections become more representative and democracy more robust.

📖 Definition Bank — Chapter 7 (likhne ke liye ready)
Election — process of exercising the right to vote and fulfilling our responsibility as citizens.
Direct elections — citizens directly choose their representatives (LS, VS, local bodies).
Indirect elections — representatives elect the leaders (President, VP, Rajya Sabha).
Psephology — scientific study of elections (Greek psēphos = pebble).
Electoral system — method of converting votes cast into seats in the legislature.
FPTP — plurality system; candidate with most votes wins (Lok Sabha, Vidhan Sabha).
Proportional Representation — seats in proportion to votes (RS, President, VP).
Single Transferable Vote (STV) — preference voting; quota = valid votes ÷ (seats + 1).
Majority system — needs >50%; otherwise a second round between the top two.
RP Act 1950 — seat allocation, delimitation, electoral rolls, universal voting at 18+.
RP Act 1951 — conduct of elections, campaigns, disputes, corrupt practices.
Delimitation — fixing number of seats and boundaries of constituencies (Art 82; 1952, 1963, 1973, 2002).
ECI — autonomous permanent constitutional body (25 Jan 1950); Art 324–329; superintendence, direction, control.
CEO / DEO / RO / ERO — state and district election machinery officers.
Electoral roll — constituency-wise voter list; only listed citizens can vote.
SIR — Special Intensive Revision; updating, verifying, correcting electoral rolls.
National Voters' Day — 25 January, with the voters' pledge.
ETPBS — electronically transmitted postal ballot for service voters.
cVIGIL — app to report MCC violations; flying squads respond.
VVPAT — Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail; printed slip verifying the vote.
MCC — Model Code of Conduct; election-time rules for parties and government.
Defection — leaving the party on whose ticket one was elected.
Anti-Defection Law — 1985, 52nd Amendment; quitting party or defying whip → disqualification; decided by Speaker/Chairman.
RUPP — Registered Unrecognised Political Parties.
