T8Types of Democracy — 4 Models 🌍☐ revised
📖 Definition
Democracy does not offer a single model of government — different societies institutionalise it in diverse ways, depending on how citizens participate in decision-making and how political power is structured.
💡 Samajh (Hinglish)
School wala example yaad karo: Team A (sab students vote karte hain bade decisions pe — direct democracy lite), Team B (class rep chunta hai president — indirect/representative), Team C (students president + council dono chunte hain, president boss — presidential feel), Team D (selected candidates hi contest kar sakti hain, president ke paas saara power — democracy sirf naam ka!). Ye hi duniya ke 4 models hain. India = Parliamentary (PM + council of ministers, accountable to legislature), USA = Presidential, Switzerland = Direct!
🌳 System — Types of Democracy (iske under kya)
  • Direct Democracy — e.g., Switzerland
    • Citizens directly participate in most decision-making processes
    • Some features of representative democracy also exist
    • Difficult to follow in large countries
  • Representative (Indirect) Democracy — e.g., India
    • People elect representatives; they do not directly govern
    • Periodic elections; government accountable to the people
    • Two sub-models:
      • Parliamentary — e.g., India, Canada: executive members are part of the legislature; executive accountable to the legislature; people elect the legislature, not the executive
      • Presidential — e.g., USA: executive is independent of the legislature; President elected by the people, accountable to the people

  • Federalism — a system in which power and responsibilities are shared between the central and state governments, as expressed in the central, state and concurrent lists; residual power remains with the central government.

T9India's Living Democracy — Scale + Women + Grassroots 📊☐ revised
💡 Samajh (Hinglish)
India ki democracy "living" kyun? Scale dekho: 96.8 crore voters, ek MP ~25 lakh logon ko represent karta hai, 22 scheduled languages me elections, 1 million+ polling stations — jungle, pahad, desert, island sab me (kahin kahin 1 voter ke liye bhi booth!). Women ke case me India ahead tha — Britain (1928) aur USA (1920) me women ne vote ke liye ladhna pada, but India ne 1950 me hi sabko Universal Adult Franchise de diya, bina ladai! Aur grassroots pe Panchayati Raj — villages me hi self-government (village = unit of grassroots democracy).
🌳 System — Facts that make India's democracy "living"
  • Scale: 96.8 crore+ registered voters (2024) — more people vote in India than the total population of many countries
  • Representation: on average one MP represents about 25 lakh people
  • Languages: elections conducted in 22 scheduled languages; election symbols let even those who cannot read vote independently
  • Reach: over one million polling stations (mountains, forests, deserts, islands) — sometimes for a single voter
  • Parties: over 2,800 registered political parties — social and regional diversity, many viewpoints compete peacefully
✍️ Exam Points — women & grassroots (Articles important!)
  • Women & the right to vote: Britain — full voting rights only in 1928; USA — 1920, after decades of protests. In India, the Constitution (in force 1950) extended Universal Adult Franchise to all citizens — men and women alike, without prolonged battles — though social prejudices still limit women's equal representation.
  • Article 243(d) — not less than one-third seats reserved for women in every Panchayat (by direct election); 21 States + 2 UTs have gone to 50% in Panchayati Raj Institutions.
  • Article 243(t) — not less than one-third for women in every Municipality; as of 2023, 17 States + 1 UT have 50% in Urban Local Bodies.
  • Three-tier system: Union, State and Local governments; Autonomous District Councils (ADCs) in north-eastern regions (legislative + judicial authority to preserve tribal customs); PESA Act, 1996 — Panchayats Extension to Scheduled Areas, empowers the Gram Sabha as the primary decision-making body.
  • Case studies: Jethipura Gram Panchayat (Gujarat) — Nanaji Deshmukh Rashtriya Gaurav Gram Sabha Puraskar, 2019 (special Gram Sabhas, Mahila Sabhas, quorum, SC/ST & women's participation); South Manubankul Gram Panchayat (Tripura) — recognised as a "women-friendly panchayat" by the Ministry of Panchayati Raj.
  • Quorum — the minimum number of members needed in an assembly meeting to make proceedings valid; Cooperatives — groups formed by people to work together for common benefit, sharing profits and responsibilities equally.
  • Participatory democracy — citizens' involvement beyond elections: local councils, public consultations, community-driven initiatives. Grassroots leaders: Chaudhary Charan Singh, Babu Jagjivan Ram (advocated for the rural poor and marginalised).
T10Challenges + Emergency (1975–77) ⚠️☐ revised
📖 Definition
Emergency — a special constitutional provision; Articles 352, 356 and 360 of the Constitution deal with National Emergency, President's Rule, and Financial Emergency respectively.
💡 Samajh (Hinglish)
Democracy ko maintenance chahiye! Challenges: illiteracy, fake news (social media se failti hai), poverty, regionalism, gender inequality, social discrimination, aur laws ke implementation me gaps. Sabse bada test tha Emergency 1975–77: Indira Gandhi ki government ke against bhaari protest (unemployment, inflation, misgovernance) → June 1975 me National Emergency lag gayi (ground: internal disturbance) → Fundamental Rights suspend, press censorship, neta activists jail me! Jayaprakash Narayan (Lok Nayak) ne Bihar-Gujarat me students aur citizens mobilise kiye. 1977 me Emergency hatai, elections hue, ruling government haari — proof ki democracy ki asli taqat: janta boss hai!
✍️ Exam Points
  • Democracy requires constant care, awareness, and active participation from both institutions and citizens.
  • Actions like damaging public property, spreading misinformation, or violating public rules weaken democratic values; indifference towards public issues also hurts democracy.
  • Emergency timeline: early 1970s — public dissatisfaction (rising unemployment, inflation, allegations of misgovernance) → June 1975: National Emergency imposed on grounds of internal disturbance → Fundamental Rights suspended, press censored, political leaders and activists arrested → mass movements led by Jayaprakash Narayan (Lok Nayak), especially in Bihar and Gujarat → 1977: Emergency lifted, general elections held → ruling government defeated.
  • The episode showed both the vulnerabilities and the resilience of Indian democracy — the power of the ballot.
  • Civic responsibility — respecting others' rights, using social media responsibly, abiding by laws, appreciating diversity, participating in activities that strengthen unity.
T11Democracy and You — Young Citizen's Toolkit 🎒☐ revised
💡 Samajh (Hinglish)
Tum bhi democracy ka part ho! Formula: informed raho (newspaper, news, responsible internet use — fake news se door!), Constitution samjho, fundamental duties follow karo, school aur community me leadership lo, aur nation-building activities join karo — NSS, NCC, Bharat Scouts & Guides. Chhote steps bhi count karte hain — democracy sirf election day nahi, roz ka practice hai!
✍️ Exam Points — Before we move on (book summary)
  • Democracy is a system of self-governance involving institutions, rules and practices for participation at local, state and national levels — not limited to elections alone.
  • Democratic processes are guided by transparency, participation, inclusion, fairness, equality, accountability, and respect for diverse opinions.
  • Democratic ideas in India have deep roots in ancient traditions (Sabhā and Samiti in Vedic texts) — consultation and collective decision-making.
  • India's democracy rests on the Constitution: popular sovereignty, universal adult franchise, fundamental rights and duties, rule of law, separation of powers.
  • India is the world's largest democracy due to the scale of citizen participation — though challenges remain in deeper inclusion and representation.
  • Citizens contribute through civic awareness, responsible media use, community participation, and respect for democratic values in everyday life.
  • Programmes like NSS and NCC instil civic responsibility, social awareness and respect for democratic ideals.

📖 Definition Bank — Chapter 6 (likhne ke liye ready)
Democracy — form of government where power lies with the citizens, who elect representatives through free and fair elections.
Popular sovereignty — the state derives its authority from the people; ultimate power lies with them.
Universal Adult Franchise — right of every citizen 18+ to vote by secret ballot.
Rule of law — no one is above the law; equality before law + equal protection of law.
Fundamental Rights — six enforceable, indivisible, inalienable rights (Art 14–32) safeguarding freedom and dignity.
Separation of powers — legislature makes laws, executive implements, judiciary interprets.
Checks and balances — each organ checks the others, preventing concentration of power.
PIL — Public Interest Litigation; legal action for enforcement of public interest.
Civil society — voluntary groups, NGOs and community associations within society.
Multi-party system — several parties contest; >50% seats forms the government, rest = opposition.
Question Hour — first hour of a parliamentary session for members' questions to the government.
Federalism — power shared between central and state governments (central/state/concurrent lists).
Sovereignty — supreme, final, independent legal authority of a State (Latin: superanus).
Direct democracy — citizens directly participate in most decision-making (Switzerland).
Representative democracy — people elect representatives to govern (India).
Quorum — minimum members required to validate assembly proceedings.
Cooperatives — groups working together for common benefit, sharing profits and responsibilities.
ADCs — Autonomous District Councils in north-east; legislative & judicial authority for tribal customs.
PESA Act 1996 — extends Panchayats to Scheduled Areas; Gram Sabha = primary decision-making body.
Emergency — Art 352 (National), Art 356 (President's Rule), Art 360 (Financial).
Civic responsibility — respecting rights, responsible social media, obeying laws, strengthening unity.
Participatory democracy — citizen engagement beyond elections: councils, consultations, community initiatives.
