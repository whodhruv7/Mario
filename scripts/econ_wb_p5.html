T1Demand — Chahna + Khareed Paana 🛒☐ revised
📖 Definition
Demand — the quantity of a product that people are willing AND able to buy at a particular price, depending on their needs, preferences, season, trend and income.
💡 Samajh (Hinglish)
Sirf chahna demand nahi hai! Ferrari chahiye sabko 🏎️ but khareedne ki purchasing power kisi ke paas nahi. Demand = desire + ability to pay. Aur ye price pe depend karti hai — sasta hai toh zyada log khareedte hain, mehnga hai toh kam. Isi ko agla topic me law me dekhte hain.
✍️ Exam Points
  • Demand = willingness + ability (purchasing power) to buy at a particular price.
  • It is not just desire — desire without purchasing power is not demand.
  • Depends on: needs, preferences, season, trend and income.
  • Purchasing power — a measure of how much one unit of currency can buy at a particular time.
T2Law of Demand — Utaavla Seesaw 📉☐ revised
📖 Definition
Law of Demand — as the price of a product rises, its quantity demanded falls; as price falls, quantity demanded rises — an inverse relationship (other factors constant).
💡 Samajh (Hinglish)
Aam (mango) season shuru — ₹150/kg → Srivalli sirf 1 kg. Supply badhi, price ₹100 → 2 kg. Full season me ₹50 → 3 kg! 🥭 Matlab: price neeche, demand upar — ulta relation. Isko graph pe draw karo toh curve neeche ki taraf jhukti hai (downward sloping).
✍️ Exam Points
  • Price ↑ → quantity demanded ↓; price ↓ → quantity demanded ↑ — inverse relationship.
  • Individual demand — quantity one consumer wants to buy at different prices (other factors constant).
  • The table of price–quantity is the demand schedule; drawn on a graph it becomes the demand curve (DD').
  • The demand curve slopes downward from left to right.

T3Individual vs Market Demand + Curve 📊☐ revised
📖 Definitions
Individual demand — quantity one consumer buys at different prices.  |  Market demand — the sum of all individual demands at each price.
💡 Samajh (Hinglish)
Srivalli + Alex + Israt — teeno ki demand add karo = market demand. 🧮 ₹50 pe: Srivalli 3 + Alex 6 + Israt 9 = 18 kg. Ek interesting baat (book me bhi): market demand curve individual curve se flatter hoti hai — kyunki ek price change pe itne saare log react karte hain ki total quantity response bada hota hai!
✍️ Exam Points
  • Market demand = Q1 + Q2 + Q3 + ... (sum of all individual demands at each price).
  • Plotting market demand at different prices gives the market demand curve (DmDm').
  • The market demand curve is flatter than an individual curve — the same price change creates a larger total quantity response (₹150→₹50: Srivalli +2 kg, but market +12 kg).

T4Demand ke Determinants (price ke alawa) 🎛️☐ revised
💡 Samajh (Hinglish)
Price same rahe, phir bhi demand badal sakti hai — kyunki aur bhi factors hain: related goods (substitute/complement), income, taste, population, season, future expectations. Jaise: coffee mehngi hui toh log chai peene lage (substitute!). Petrol mehnga toh car ki demand giri (complement!). 🚗⛽
✍️ Exam Points — sab determinants
  • Price of related goods — Substitute goods replace each other (tea ↔ coffee, mangoes ↔ bananas): price of one ↑ → demand for the other ↑. Complementary goods are used together (car + petrol, printer + cartridges, movie ticket + popcorn): price of one ↑ → demand for the other ↓.
  • Income: when household income rises, consumers buy more or better quality → quantity demanded rises (prices constant).
  • Taste & preference: personal likes decide demand — Srivalli loves mangoes and won't substitute oranges even if cheaper.
  • Population size & composition: India = most populous nation → huge domestic demand. More children → sports shoes; more working adults → formal shoes; more elderly → comfortable/orthopaedic shoes.
  • Seasonality: demand changes with weather, festivals, habits — bookshops at new session, sweets in festive season, sweaters in winter.
  • Future price expectations: expect prices to fall → postpone purchases (demand ↓ now); expect rise → buy immediately (demand ↑ now) — e.g., waiting for Diwali/New Year discounts.
📖 Bonus Definition
Diminishing marginal utility — the extra satisfaction (utility) from each additional unit keeps falling as we consume more. Pehla aam 😍, teesra aam 😐 — isliye willingness to pay bhi girti hai, aur demand price ke saath inverse me chalti hai.
T5Supply & Law of Supply 📦☐ revised
📖 Definitions
Supply — the quantity of a product that sellers are willing and able to offer at a particular price.  |  Law of Supply — as price rises, quantity supplied rises; as price falls, quantity supplied falls — a direct relationship.
💡 Samajh (Hinglish)
Demand ka ulta! 💁 Seller ki soch: price zyada → profit zyada → zyada bechuga, aur naye firms bhi market me ghuseinge. Price kam → profit kam → supply bhi kam. Seller A: ₹50 pe 1 kg, ₹100 pe 2 kg, ₹150 pe 3 kg. Isliye supply curve upar ki taraf chadhti hai (upward sloping) 📈 — demand wali seesaw ka ulta.
✍️ Exam Points
  • Supply = willingness + ability of sellers to offer a product at a particular price.
  • Law of supply (direct relationship): price ↑ → quantity supplied ↑ — higher prices increase profitability and attract new firms.
  • Individual supply — quantity one seller offers at different prices; market supply — sum of all sellers (A+B+C = QS).
  • The supply curve slopes upward (e.g., sellers A, B, C: at ₹50 supply 6 kg, ₹100 → 12 kg, ₹150 → 18 kg in total).
  • Season logic: start of mango season — supply low → prices high; mid-season — supply ↑ → prices fall.
