T1Why Elections Matter — 4 Values 🗳️☐ revised
📖 Definitions
Election — a process through which we exercise the right to vote and fulfil our responsibility as citizens.  |  Direct elections — citizens vote directly to choose their representatives or leaders.  |  Indirect elections — citizens vote for representatives who then choose/elect the leaders.
💡 Samajh (Hinglish)
Simple sawal: CR (class rep) ko bina election agle saal bhi CR rahne denge? Nahi! Wahi government ke saath hai — periodic elections democracy ka core hai. Ek hi party ho toh choice kya? Fair nahi! Election se milne wali 4 values ratto: Equality (sabki vote barabar), Representation (har awaaz pahunchti hai), Accountability (nikal sakte hain!), Legitimacy (janta ka mandate = asli license). Bonus word: psephology — Greek psēphos (pebble — ancient Greece me voting ke liye!) + logy = scientific study of elections.
🌳 System — Direct vs Indirect Elections (kisme kya)
  • Direct elections — every 5 years
    • Lok Sabha (central level)
    • Vidhan Sabha (Legislative Assembly, state level)
    • Local bodies — Panchayats, municipal corporations, etc.
  • Indirect elections
    • The President of India
    • The Vice President
    • Members of the Rajya Sabha
✍️ Exam Points
  • Regular and periodic elections lie at the core of democracy — representatives are chosen through direct or indirect elections.
  • Elected representatives are accountable to the people; our vote is the principal means of exercising this right.
  • The government cannot continue beyond its prescribed term without seeking the people's mandate again.
  • Elections give every citizen an equal voice in the formation of a government — they are the soul of democracy.
T2The Electoral System — FPTP vs Majority vs PR 🧮☐ revised
📖 Definition
Electoral system — the method of converting the votes cast into seats in the legislature; the way a system allocates seats distinguishes it from others. Our Constitution framers chose the plurality system or 'First-Past-The-Post' (FPTP).
💡 Samajh (Hinglish)
Race ka concept: FPTP = race ki finishing line pe jo first aaya, wahi winner — chahe 40% hi vote mile ho! (Haritbhumi me Party A 40% pe jeet gayi). Majority system = 50%+ chahiye, warna round 2 top-2 me (Ratnadweep me Party Y ne 55% se jeeta). PR (Proportional Representation) = party ko vote do, seats vote-share ke hisaab se (Swarnalok me 40% = 40 seats). India me Lok Sabha + Vidhan Sabha = FPTP, but Rajya Sabha + President + VP = PR. Simple!

T3Vidhan Parishad + Single Transferable Vote 🏛️☐ revised
💡 Samajh (Hinglish)
6 states me legislature ka "double floor" hai — Vidhan Parishad (Upper House, Legislative Council) + Vidhan Sabha (Lower House). Parishad ka composition interesting hai: Vidhan Sabha members + local bodies + graduates + teachers ke vote se, aur Governor ke nominated members (art, science, social service, literature, co-operatives ke log). Aur STV (Single Transferable Vote) ka logic: preference mark karo (1, 2, 3...), jo candidate quota poora kare wahi jeeta; sabse kam wala bahar, uske votes 2nd preference pe transfer!
🌳 System — Vidhan Parishad (Legislative Council) ka composition
  • Elected members
    • By members of the Vidhan Sabha
    • By local bodies
    • By graduates and teachers of the State
  • Nominated members — by the Governor, from fields like art, science, social service, literature, and co-operatives
  • Bicameral states (6): Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh
  • Elections to the Vidhan Parishad use proportional representation by single transferable vote
✍️ Exam Points — STV ka process (steps)
  1. Voters mark their preferences (1st, 2nd, 3rd...) on the ballot.
  2. First preference votes are counted for all candidates; those who reach the quota (minimum votes needed to win) are declared elected.
  3. The candidate with the lowest votes is eliminated; their votes are transferred to the second preferences marked on those ballots.
  4. The process continues till the required number of candidates are elected.
🧮 Quota formula: Quota = Total valid votes ÷ (Seats to be filled + 1) — the minimum votes required to win.
T4The Laws — RP Act 1950 & 1951 📜☐ revised
💡 Samajh (Hinglish)
Election ke 2 kadhai (main laws): RP Act 1950 = "set-up wala" — seats ka allocation, constituency boundaries (delimitation), electoral rolls banana, 18+ sabko vote ka right. RP Act 1951 = "conduct wala" — nomination, campaign, voting procedure, disputes — sab rules. Plus Presidential and Vice-Presidential Act, 1952. Corrupt practices strictly banned: rishwat (gift/promise for vote), religion/caste ke naam pe vote maangna, aur government officials ki madad lena — teeno offences hain!
🌳 System — Election Laws (kya karta hai kaunsa)
  • Representation of the People Act, 1950
    • Allocation of seats and delimitation of constituencies
    • Preparation and revision of electoral rolls
    • Ensuring every citizen above 18 years can vote without discrimination
  • Representation of the People Act, 1951
    • All other aspects of the conduct of elections and post-election disputes
    • Rules for nomination of candidates, election campaigns, voting procedures, and dispute resolution
    • Lists electoral offences and corrupt practices
  • The Presidential and Vice-Presidential Act, 1952
  • Corrupt practices (banned): bribery (gift/offer/promise to make someone contest, withdraw or vote); appealing for votes on religion, caste, race, community or language; taking help of government personnel (gazetted officers, judges, magistrates, armed forces, police, excise officers)
✍️ Exam Points
  • Together these Acts ensure the integrity, transparency and accountability of India's electoral process.
T5Delimitation Commission 🗺️☐ revised
📖 Definition
Delimitation — the process of determining the number of seats and fixing the territorial boundaries in each state for elections to the Lok Sabha and the Vidhan Sabha, so that the ratio of seats to population is as close to equal as possible across all constituencies.
💡 Samajh (Hinglish)
Constituency ki boundaries permanent nahi hain! Population shift hoti rehti hai — bina delimitation ke ek MP 5 lakh logon ko represent karta aur doosra 25 lakh ko... unfair na? Isliye time-time pe boundaries re-draw hoti hain. Article 82 ke under Delimitation Commission banta hai; India me 4 commissions hue hain: 1952, 1963, 1973, 2002. (1973 aur 2002 ke beech bada gap — population control se related interesting story!)
✍️ Exam Points
  • Boundaries of parliamentary and assembly constituencies change over time depending on shifts in population density.
  • Without delimitation, equal representation is defeated — one MP might represent five lakh people while another represents twenty-five lakh.
  • Article 82 of the Constitution mandates the establishment of a Delimitation Commission.
  • India has had four Delimitation Commissions: 1952, 1963, 1973 and 2002.
