📝Big Questions — Exam-Ready Answers (Ch 7)
Q1. Why are elections essential in a democratic system?
Answer
  • Elections are one of the most important processes for exercising democratic rights — regular and periodic elections lie at the core of democracy.
  • They allow people to choose those who make decisions on their behalf; representatives are chosen through direct or indirect elections.
  • Elected representatives are accountable to the people — the right to elect and hold them accountable is ensured through periodic elections.
  • Our vote is the principal means of exercising this right.
  • Elections provide equality (every vote equal), representation (every voice counts), accountability (non-performers can be changed) and legitimacy (people's mandate).
  • A government cannot continue beyond its prescribed term without seeking the people's mandate again.
Q2. Explain the First-Past-The-Post system and proportional representation with examples.
Answer
  • FPTP (plurality system): each voter votes for one candidate in the constituency; the candidate with the most votes wins, even with less than 50% — e.g., in a 3-party contest, Party A with 40% forms the government.
  • We elect members of the Lok Sabha and Vidhan Sabha using FPTP.
  • Proportional Representation: voters vote for a party, not a person; seats are allotted in proportion to total votes received — e.g., parties with 40%, 35%, 15%, 10% votes get seats in the same proportion.
  • Elections to the Rajya Sabha, the President and the Vice President use proportional representation (STV for the Vidhan Parishad/Rajya Sabha).
  • A majority system is the third type: a candidate needs more than 50%; otherwise the top two contest a second round.
Q3. What laws govern the conduct of elections in India? Explain their roles.
Answer
  • The Representation of the People Act, 1950 — deals mainly with the allocation of seats, delimitation of constituencies, and the preparation and revision of electoral rolls; ensures every citizen above 18 years has the right to vote without discrimination.
  • The Representation of the People Act, 1951 — covers all other aspects of the conduct of elections and post-election disputes: nomination of candidates, campaigns, voting procedures and dispute resolution.
  • It also lists electoral offences and corrupt practices: bribery; appealing for votes on religion, caste, race, community or language; using government personnel (gazetted officers, judges, armed forces, police, excise).
  • The Presidential and Vice-Presidential Act, 1952 also governs related elections.
  • Together these Acts ensure the integrity, transparency and accountability of India's electoral process.
Q4. What is delimitation? Why is it necessary?
Answer
  • Delimitation is the process of determining the number of seats and fixing the territorial boundaries in each state for elections to the Lok Sabha and the Vidhan Sabha.
  • It aims to keep the ratio of seats to population as equal as possible across all constituencies.
  • Boundaries of constituencies are not permanently fixed — they change with shifts in population density.
  • Without delimitation, one MP might represent five lakh people while another represents twenty-five lakh — defeating equal representation.
  • Article 82 mandates a Delimitation Commission; India has had four — in 1952, 1963, 1973 and 2002.
Q5. Describe the Election Commission of India and its functions.
Answer
  • The ECI is an autonomous, permanent constitutional body established on 25 January 1950; Articles 324–329 define its powers and duties.
  • The Constitution vests in the ECI the superintendence, direction and control of the entire election process.
  • It conducts elections to the Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha, Vidhan Sabha, Vidhan Parishad, the President and the Vice President.
  • Functions: (i) creates and revises electoral rolls (enumerators, SIR, claims & objections); (ii) decides election schedules considering weather, agricultural cycles, exams and festivals; (iii) registers political parties, allots symbols and classifies them (national/state/RUPP), acting as a quasi-judicial body in disputes; (iv) ensures free, fair and inclusive elections (PwDs, 85+ home voting, Braille EVMs, transgender and PVTG inclusion).
  • At the state level, the Chief Electoral Officer supervises election work; DMs, SDMs and Tahsildars serve as DEOs, ROs and EROs.
Q6. What is the Special Intensive Revision (SIR)? Why is it necessary?
Answer
  • SIR is an ECI exercise of updating, verifying and correcting the electoral rolls.
  • It ensures that no eligible citizen is left out and no ineligible person is included in the roll.
  • It adds all voters — especially young voters who have just turned 18 and may be left out due to lack of awareness.
  • It deletes names on the basis of death of the voter, change of residence, duplicate enrolment, and being permanently untraceable.
  • The ECI also gives time to raise claims and objections against the revised roll and settles them before publishing the final roll.
Q7. What role do political parties play in elections and democracy?
Answer
  • Political parties are essential to a democratic system — they work directly at the grassroots level.
  • They enable voters to make informed decisions during elections.
  • They organise public opinion, campaign on specific issues, and offer voters meaningful choices.
  • Voters learn about candidates, their policies, vision and positions on issues primarily through parties (rallies, manifestos, media debates).
  • The winning party forms the government — formulating and implementing policies and ensuring accountability; other parties form the opposition, participating in debates and committees.
  • They provide governance, accountability, representation and public engagement — helping democracy function effectively.
Q8. What is defection? Explain the Anti-Defection Law.
Answer
  • Defection — abandoning or switching from the political party under whose banner a candidate was elected; joining another party or acting independently of party decisions (like voting in the legislature).
  • Seen as political opportunism in the context of party discipline and stability — though it can also uphold conscience or respond to changing public expectations.
  • The Anti-Defection Law was passed in 1985 through the 52nd Constitutional Amendment Act.
  • Purpose: prevent defection and provide stability to the elected government.
  • If a member of Parliament or a State Assembly voluntarily gives up party membership or votes against the party's whip, the member can be disqualified from the House.
  • The Speaker or Chairman of the House decides such cases.
Q9. How are political parties recognised as national or state parties? Mention the criteria.
Answer
  • The ECI registers and recognises political parties based on specific criteria.
  • National party (any one condition): secures not less than 6% of valid votes in four or more states plus wins 4 Lok Sabha seats; OR wins at least 2% of Lok Sabha seats with candidates elected from at least three states; OR is recognised as a state party in at least four states.
  • State party (any one condition): 6% of valid votes in the state Assembly election + at least 2 Assembly seats; OR 6% of LS votes from the state + 1 LS seat; OR at least 3% of Assembly seats (or 3 seats); OR 1 LS seat per 25 seats allotted to the state; OR 8% of valid votes polled in the state.
  • RUPP: registered-unrecognised parties — those with insufficient vote shares or that have never contested since registration.
  • National and state parties are allotted election symbols; the ECI insists on inner-party democracy (periodic organisational elections).
Q10. What are the challenges to free and fair elections? How does the ECI address them?
Answer
  • Conducting elections for over 96.8 crore voters across thousands of polling stations, hundreds of parties and diverse socio-economic realities is a challenging task.
  • Major challenges: misinformation, fake news, and intimidation of voters.
  • The ECI addresses them through the Representation of the People Acts (1950 and 1951) — laws covering offences and corrupt practices.
  • The Model Code of Conduct (MCC) regulates party behaviour during campaigns; EVMs and VVPAT (Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail) ensure transparent voting and counting.
  • Voters' awareness campaigns and inclusive initiatives (Braille EVMs, home voting for 85+ and PwDs, ETPBS for service voters) push the motto 'No Voter to Be Left Behind'.
  • With constant vigilance and active citizen participation, elections become more representative and democracy more robust.
