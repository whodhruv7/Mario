Democracy
One line: How democracy evolved in India — from ancient collective decision-making traditions to the world's largest constitutional democracy — its principles, forms, practices and challenges.
⚡ 5-Minute Revision💡 Hinglish📜 Articles & Dates🔥 Must-Remember Facts📝 Answer Guide
1Chapter at a Glance
  • Democracy = government in which the source of power & authority lies with the citizens, who elect representatives through free & fair elections; founded on freedom, equality, justice, rights & duties
  • Big questions: How has the idea of democracy evolved? What are its forms in India & the world? What are its essential features beyond institutions? What challenges does Indian democracy face?
  • India = world's largest democracy 🔥: population 140+ crore, voters 96.8 crore (2024)
  • Constitution 🔥: adopted 26 November 1949, in force 26 January 1950; guarantees Fundamental Rights; no discrimination on religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth
2Key Terms — one-line meanings
Popular sovereignty — ultimate source of power = the people
Universal Adult Franchise — every citizen 18+ votes by secret ballot, no discrimination
Rule of Law — no one above the law; disputes by legal procedures, not force
Equality before law — every individual treated equally by law
Equal protection of law — all in similar circumstances treated equally
Procedure established by law — no punishment without due process/statute
Separation of Powers — legislature makes, executive implements, judiciary interprets
Judicial review — judiciary can declare a law unconstitutional
PIL — Public Interest Litigation; court action for public interest
Civil society — voluntary groups, NGOs, community associations
Federalism — power shared centre ↔ states (Union, State, Concurrent lists)
Sovereignty — from Latin superanus ("supreme"); supreme final authority of a State
Question Hour — first hour of a session; members question the government
Quorum — minimum members needed for valid proceedings
Cooperatives — groups for common benefit; shared profits & responsibilities
Participatory democracy — engagement beyond elections (local councils, consultations)
Emergency — Articles 352 (National), 356 (President's Rule), 360 (Financial)
Civic responsibility — respect others' rights, responsible social media, abide by laws
3Democratic Traditions — India's Long Story 📌
VEDIC ROOTSsabhā, samiti, vidhata — collective decision-making; early republics (gaṇas/saṁghas); king ruled in consultation with assemblies & ministers
BUDDHIST SAṀGHAdebate & discussion; members chose their leader & voted on decisions
VEDIC VERSEṚig Veda 10.191.3 (Aikyamatya Sūktam): "Their counsel is one and the same; their assembly is one…" — consultation & consensus
COLONIAL SETBACKinvasions disrupted political fabric; by 19th century the British colonised most of the subcontinent, limiting people's participation
FREEDOM STRUGGLErevived & strengthened democratic ideas among the people
CONSTITUTIONConstituent Assembly formed 1946; 2 years 11 months 18 days → world's longest written Constitution; Dr. B.R. Ambedkar = Chairman of the Drafting Committee 🔥; CAD wanted a dynamic document → Article 368 (amendments) — "robust, flexible, transformative, responsive"; global democratic values also influenced the CAD
47 Principles of Democracy — non-negotiable 🔥 (whole section)
PrincipleMeaning + key pointsExample / why it matters
1 Popular sovereigntyUltimate power = people; govt elected via free & fair electionsUniversal Adult Franchise (18+, secret ballot) — no ruler without people's consent
2 Rule of lawNo one above law; equality before + equal protection of law; court procedures, due processEven a minister/PM can be tried — prevents misuse of power
3 Fundamental Rights6 FRs safeguarding rights, freedom, dignity; enforceable; indivisible & inalienable; reasonable restrictionsArticle 21A — Right to Education (added 2009): free & compulsory, ages 6–14
4 Separation of powersLegislature–executive–judiciary; checks & balances; Parliament can amend but judiciary reviewsJudiciary = impartial guardian of the Constitution; PIL = access to justice
5 Accountability & transparencyGovts answerable via elections, public debates, civil societyRTI Act 2005; Question Hour; auditing of accounts
6 Multi-party systemSeveral parties contest; >50% seats = government, rest = opposition; function under RP Act 1951India: 2,800+ registered parties — real choice
7 Safeguarding vulnerable groupsProtect all communities; resources distributed equitablyArticle 46: special care for weaker sections, SCs & STs
  • The 6 FRs with articles 🔥: Equality (14–18) • Freedom (19–22) • Against Exploitation (23–24) • Freedom of Religion (25–28) • Cultural & Educational (29–30) • Constitutional Remedies (32); remedies via 32 & 226 ⚠️ favourite exam list
  • FRs (enforceable rights) ≠ Fundamental Duties (responsibilities) ≠ DPSP (guidelines for the State) ⚠️ classic confusion

6Types of Democracy 🔥 + world examples 📌
TypeKey featuresExample
DirectCitizens directly make most decisions; difficult in large countriesSwitzerland
Representative (indirect)Elect representatives; periodic elections; govt accountableIndia
ParliamentaryExecutive part of & accountable to legislature; people elect legislature, NOT executiveIndia, Canada, UK
PresidentialExecutive independent of legislature; President elected by peopleUSA
  • No single model — societies institutionalise democracy differently; but everywhere: freedom, equality, participation, rule of law, rights, duties
  • Countries: India (President = Head of State + PM = Head of Govt; written constitution, federalism) • Canada (Governor-General + PM) • UK (constitutional monarchy, UNWRITTEN constitution) • Switzerland (Federal Council) • USA (President = both heads, two major parties) 📌
7Democracy in Practice — India's Living Democracy
  • Huge representation: 1 MP ≈ 25 lakh people 📌; elections in 22 scheduled languages; election symbols let non-literate citizens vote independently
  • Over 1 million polling stations — mountains, forests, deserts, islands; sometimes a station for a single voter; 2,800+ parties compete peacefully
  • Three-tier system: Union → State → Local (Panchayati Raj & Municipalities)
  • Tribal protection ⭐: Autonomous District Councils (ADCs) in the north-east (legislative + judicial powers to preserve tribal customs); PESA Act 1996 — Gram Sabha = primary decision-making body in Scheduled Areas
🔥 CASE 1 — JETHIPURA GRAM PANCHAYAT (Gujarat)
Won Nanaji Deshmukh Rashtriya Gaurav Gram Sabha Puraskar (NDRGGSP) 2019 — exemplary Gram Sabhas, records, Special Gram Sabhas, Mahila Sabhas, action on education/sanitation/welfare; quorum with active SC, ST & women participation.
🔥 CASE 2 — SOUTH MANUBANKUL (Tripura)
National-level "women-friendly panchayat" (Ministry of Panchayati Raj) — women in decision-making, health & livelihood facilities, self-help groups; democracy goes far beyond voting.
8Women and Democracy 🔥
CountryWomen got full voting rights
BritainOnly in 1928 — after decades of protest
USAOnly in 1920 — after long struggles
IndiaFrom the very start — 1950, Universal Adult Franchise to ALL, no legal battles needed 📌
  • BUT right to vote ≠ equal participation — social prejudices still limit women's political representation
  • Article 243D 📌: not less than ⅓ seats for women in every Panchayat; 21 States + 2 UTs raised it to 50%
  • Article 243T 📌: ⅓ for women in every Municipality; 17 States + 1 UT at 50% (as of 2023); empowerment starts with participation in decision-making
  • Grassroots leaders for rural poor & marginalised: Chaudhary Charan Singh & Babu Jagjivan Ram ⭐
9The Right to Information (RTI) Act, 2005 🔥
  • How it became law (democratic process in action): citizens, journalists, civil society & elected representatives raised concerns → parliamentary committees → expert groups → Parliament debate → consultations → safeguards for national security & efficiency → passed → in force 2005
  • What it does: empowers citizens to seek information from government departments → accountability & democratic governance; civil society + community institutions = bridges between citizens & the State (PIL, campaigns)
10Challenges to Indian Democracy
  • Democracy needs constant care, awareness & participation — from institutions AND citizens; rights must be exercised responsibly
  • Everyday weakening: damaging public property, misinformation/fake news (social media!), violating public rules, indifference
  • Major challenges 📌: illiteracy, misinformation, inequality, poverty, regionalism, gender inequality, social discrimination, gaps in implementation (reduces trust)
BACKGROUNDEarly 1970s: dissatisfaction with Indira Gandhi's government — unemployment, inflation, misgovernance allegations → protests
WHAT HAPPENEDJune 1975: National Emergency imposed on grounds of internal disturbance (Articles 352, 356, 360 = Emergency provisions)
EFFECTSMajority of Fundamental Rights suspended; press censored; leaders & activists arrested; democratic institutions under severe strain
RESISTANCEMass movements by Jayaprakash Narayan (Lok Nayak) — mobilised students & citizens, esp. Bihar & Gujarat
RESULT & LESSONLifted 1977 → elections → government defeated by ballot. Showed vulnerability AND resilience of democracy + importance of constitutional safeguards & active citizens 🔥
11Democracy and You — Young Citizens
  • Being well-informed = vital responsibility — newspapers, news, responsible internet use
  • Civic programmes ⭐: NSS (National Service Scheme), NCC (National Cadet Corps), Bharat Scouts & Guides — service, teamwork, democratic ideals
  • Youth: know the Constitution, follow Fundamental Duties, be media literate, lead in school & community — democracy = continuous process, strength = informed responsible citizens 💡
12💡 Hinglish Me Samjho — difficult concepts, ek line mein
Democracy ke Indian roots: Britishers se pehle hi — sabhā-samiti, Buddha ki Saṁgha mein voting, gaṇa-saṁgha republics. Freedom struggle ne values dobara jagayi.
FR vs Duties vs DPSP: FR = tumhare HAQ (court mein enforceable); Duties = tumhara FARZ; DPSP = SARKAR ke guidelines (welfare goals, enforceable nahi) — exam ka favourite confusion!
Parliamentary vs Presidential: India mein PM Parliament ke andar se (confidence vote pe answerable — tum MP ko vote dete ho, PM ko nahi); USA mein President seedha logon ke vote se.
PESA & ADC: ADC = north-east tribal areas ka apna chhota sarkar (customs bachao); PESA 1996 = Scheduled Areas mein Gram Sabha hi decision-maker. Dono ka goal: tribal heritage + self-governance.
RTI ka matlab: "right to question" — koi bhi citizen sarkari department se poochhe "kitna kharch hua?", sarkar ko jawaab dena padta hai. Corruption pakadne ka powerful hathiyar.
Emergency kya thi: 1975–77 rights suspend + press censorship + leaders jail; JP (Lok Nayak) ne andolan uthaya; 1977 mein janta ne ballot se jawab diya — democracy resilient hai!

16📝 How to Use These Points in an Answer
  • Formula: Introduction (1–2 lines — define the concept) → Body (3–5 numbered points: keyword + article/example) → Conclusion (1 line judgement). Underline articles & dates.
  • Q: "What are the essential principles of democracy in India?" (5m): Intro = thriving democracy rests on non-negotiable principles upheld by constitutional provisions. Body = ① popular sovereignty + UAF (18+, secret ballot) ② rule of law — no one above law ③ 6 FRs (Articles 14–32) ④ separation of powers — checks & balances, judicial review ⑤ accountability & transparency — RTI 2005, Question Hour + Article 46. Conclusion = power remains with the people, government stays accountable.
  • Q: "Is voting alone enough to make a country democratic? Justify." (5m): Intro = voting is essential but democracy needs much more. Body = ① free & fair elections + independent ECI ② rule of law + FRs protected by judiciary ③ accountability — RTI, Question Hour, free press ④ participation beyond voting — Gram Sabhas, civil society, PIL ⑤ counter-example: Emergency 1975–77 — elections alone could not stop rights suspension; citizen vigilance needed. Conclusion = democracy is a continuous process needing informed citizens + strong institutions, not just ballot boxes.
