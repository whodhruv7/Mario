   207→Separation of Powers
   208→The separation of powers among the legislature, executive, and
   209→judiciary—each with distinct responsibilities—enables democratic
   210→institutions to function effectively and responsibly. The legislature
   211→makes laws, the executive implements them, and the judiciary
   212→interprets them, thereby preventing the concentration of power in
   213→any one organ. This separation is essential for maintaining checks and
   214→balances among the three organs of the government. For example,
   215→while parliament has the power to amend the Constitution, the
   216→judiciary can review such amendments to ensure they are consistent
   217→with the spirit of the Constitution. Similarly, the executive power of
   218→enforcement agencies is regulated by laws and rules framed by the
   219→Parliament and can also be reviewed by competent courts. If a law is
   220→inconsistent with the constitutional provisions, the judiciary has the
   221→power to declare it unconstitutional.
