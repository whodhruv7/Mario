    18→We have learnt in previous grades that democracy is a form of 
    19→government in which the source of power and authority lies with 
    20→the citizens of the country, who elect their representatives through 
    21→free and fair elections. Democracy is founded on the principles of 
    22→freedom, equality, justice, rights and duties, ensuring that citizens 
    23→have the right to vote, express their opinions, and participate in the 
    24→decision-making processes.
    25→India is the world’s largest democracy. With a population of 
    26→over 140 crore and a voter base of over 96.8 crore (in 2024), India, 
    27→that is, Bharat, represents the largest participatory democracy, where 
    28→people choose their government by electing their representatives. 
    29→Our democracy serves as a beacon of hope and an example for the rest 
    30→of the world. However, like all democracies, India also faces certain 
    31→challenges, some of which we will examine later in this chapter. 
    32→A constitution plays a crucial role in safeguarding the rights of the 
    33→people in a democracy. In India, democratic principles are guided by 
    34→the Constitution of India, which was adopted on 26 November 1949 
    35→and came into force on 26 January 1950. The Constitution not only 
    36→guarantees but also protects the Fundamental Rights of all citizens. 
    37→It ensures that no citizen is discriminated against on the basis of 
    38→religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth. It also encourages active 
    39→citizen participation in democratic processes at the national, state, 
    40→and local levels through  elections.

   337→The Rule of Law is a fundamental principle of any democratic 
   338→society. It ensures equality before the law and equal protection of 
   339→the law, and establishes that no one is above the law. When laws are 
   340→violated, disputes are resolved in a court of law through procedures 
   341→established by law not by force or personal influence. This prevents 
   342→misuse of power and protects people’s rights. No citizen can be 
   343→punished without due process, and everyone is provided a fair 
   344→opportunity to defend their rights against arbitrary action. In this 
   345→way, the rule of law promotes justice, accountability, and trust in the 
   346→democratic system.

   348→The Fundamental Rights enshrined in the Indian Constitution play 
   349→a crucial role in safeguarding the rights, freedom, and dignity of 
   350→citizens. There are six Fundamental Rights—the Right to Equality 
   351→(Articles 14–18); the Right to Freedom (Articles 19–22); the Right 
   352→Against Exploitation (Articles 23–24); the Right to Freedom of 
   353→Religion (Articles 25–28); Cultural and Educational Rights (Articles 
   354→29–30); and the Right to Constitutional Remedies (Article 32). Any 
   355→violation of these can lead to appropriate legal action, and citizens 
   356→can seek constitutional remedies under Articles 32 and 226 for 
   357→the protection and reinforcement of their fundamental rights. 
   358→These rights are enforceable in courts of law and are indivisible 
   359→and inalienable, subject to reasonable restrictions in accordance 
   360→with the procedure established by law.

   440→Separation of Powers
   441→The separation of powers among the legislature, executive, and 
   442→judiciary—each with distinct responsibilities—enables democratic 
   443→institutions to function effectively and responsibly. The legislature 
   444→makes laws, the executive implements them, and the judiciary 
   445→interprets them, thereby preventing the concentration of power in 
   446→any one organ. This separation is essential for maintaining checks and 
   447→balances among the three organs of the government. For example, 
   448→while parliament has the power to amend the Constitution, the 
   449→judiciary can review such amendments to ensure they are consistent 
   450→with the spirit of the Constitution. Similarly, the executive power of 
   451→enforcement agencies is regulated by laws and rules framed by the 
   452→Parliament and can also be reviewed by competent courts. If a law is 
   453→inconsistent with the constitutional provisions, the judiciary has the 
   454→power to declare it unconstitutional.
   455→The judiciary upholds the Constitution and plays a vital role in 
   456→protecting and promoting democratic values and the rights of all 
   457→sections of the society. It ensures the laws enacted by the Parliament 
   458→of India and the State Legislative Assemblies are in accordance with 
   459→the Constitution. The judiciary is an impartial and independent 
   460→institution that safeguards citizens’ rights and upholds the spirit 
   461→of the Constitution. Important initiatives, such as Public Interest 
   462→Litigation (PIL), have also been taken by the judiciary from time to 
   463→time to ensure access to justice for all.

   542→Multi-Party System
   543→In a multi-party system, several political parties contest elections, 
   544→representing diverse voices and interests. This gives people the 
   545→power to choose those they consider most suitable. Each political 
   546→party is guided by certain ideas, values, and principles that shape its 
   547→policies and actions. In elections to the Parliament or State Assemblies, 
   548→the party—or a coalition of parties—that secures more than 50 per 
   549→cent of the seats forms the government, while those with fewer seats 
   550→constitute the opposition. Political parties represent a wide range of 
   551→sociocultural political ideological and regional interests reflecting 
   552→the diversity of Indian society. They function within the framework 
   553→of rules and regulations laid down by the Representation of the 
   554→People Act, 1951. 
   555→Safeguarding the Rights of Vulnerable Groups
   556→It is the duty of the government to protect all communities, regardless 
   557→of caste, gender, religion, or regional background. Given historical 
   558→factors that have led to an unequal distribution of opportunities and 
   559→resources, it is essential for the government to ensure that resources 
   560→are distributed equitably, especially to the most vulnerable sections 
   561→of society, without discrimination. For example, Article 46 of the 
   562→Constitution states that “the State shall promote with special care 
   563→the educational and economic interests of the weaker sections of the 
   564→people, and, in particular, of the Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled 
   565→Tribes, and shall protect them from social injustice and all forms of 
   566→exploitation.”

   580→Multi-Party System
   581→In a multi-party system, several political parties contest elections, 
   582→representing diverse voices and interests. This gives people the 
   583→power to choose those they consider most suitable. Each political 
   584→party is guided by certain ideas, values, and principles that shape its 
   585→policies and actions. In elections to the Parliament or State Assemblies, 
   586→the party—or a coalition of parties—that secures more than 50 per 
   587→cent of the seats forms the government, while those with fewer seats 
   588→constitute the opposition. Political parties represent a wide range of 
   589→sociocultural political ideological and regional interests reflecting 
   590→the diversity of Indian society. They function within the framework 
   591→of rules and regulations laid down by the Representation of the 
   592→People Act, 1951. 
   593→Safeguarding the Rights of Vulnerable Groups
   594→It is the duty of the government to protect all communities, regardless 
   595→of caste, gender, religion, or regional background. Given historical
   596→factors that have led to an unequal distribution of opportunities and 
   597→resources, it is essential for the government to ensure that resources 
   598→are distributed equitably, especially to the most vulnerable sections 
   599→of society, without discrimination. For example, Article 46 of the 
   600→Constitution states that “the State shall promote with special care 
   601→the educational and economic interests of the weaker sections of the 
   602→people, and, in particular, of the Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled
   603→Tribes, and shall protect them from social injustice and all forms of 
   604→exploitation.”
