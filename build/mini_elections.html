Elections
One line: How India runs the world's largest democratic exercise — electoral systems that convert votes into seats, the laws & institutions (ECI, Delimitation Commission) that keep elections free & fair, and the role of political parties.

1Chapter at a Glance
  • Election = process through which we exercise the right to vote & fulfil our responsibility as citizens; regular, periodic elections lie at the core of democracy 🔥
  • Big questions: Why are elections essential? How do electoral systems shape representation & fairness? What roles do institutions & laws play? What challenges remain?
  • Why elections matter (ARLE) 🔥: Accountability • Representation • Legitimacy • Equality — the vote = principal means of exercising this right
2Key Terms — one-line meanings
Direct elections — citizens vote directly (Lok Sabha, Vidhan Sabha, local bodies)
Indirect elections — representatives choose leaders (President, VP, Rajya Sabha)
Psephology — scientific study of elections; Greek psēphos = "pebble" 📌
Constituency — territorial area electing one representative
Electoral system — method of converting votes into seats
FPTP — candidate with MOST votes wins, even if <50%
Majority system — two rounds; winner needs >50%
Proportional Representation — seats in proportion to votes received
STV — voters mark preferences; votes of eliminated candidates transferred
Quota — minimum votes to win = valid votes ÷ seats to be filled
Vidhan Parishad — Legislative Council (Upper House in bicameral states)
Delimitation — fixing number of seats & constituency boundaries
Electoral roll — voters' list per constituency, polling-station-wise
SIR — Special Intensive Revision of rolls (add/delete/verify)
Defection — leaving the party whose ticket you won on
Whip — party's voting instruction; defying it can disqualify
RUPP — Registered Unrecognised Political Parties
Coalition — alliance government (first national: Janata Party, 1977)
VVPAT — printed slip so voters can verify their vote
ETPBS — service voters cast postal ballots electronically
MCC — Model Code of Conduct; violations via cVIGIL
3The Electoral System — FPTP vs Majority vs PR 🔥
  • First step of conducting elections = deciding how votes convert into seats; the Constitution's framers chose the plurality (FPTP) system
  • FPTP used for: Lok Sabha + Vidhan Sabha | PR (via STV) used for: Rajya Sabha, President, Vice President (+ Vidhan Parishad) 📌
FeatureFPTP (plurality)Majority (2 rounds)Proportional Representation
How it worksOne vote per candidate in constituency; MOST votes wins (even <50%)Needs >50%; else top two re-compete in Round 2Vote for a party, not a person; seats ∝ votes
Textbook example 📌Haritbhumi: A 40%, B 35%, C 25% → A forms govt with only 40%Ratnadeep: nobody crossed 50% in Round 1 → Round 2: Y wins with 55%Swarnalok (100 seats): M 40, N 35, O 15, P 10 seats — power shared by vote share
Who forms govtMost seats (may be <50% of votes)Round-2 majority winnerCoalitions — power shared proportionately
QUOTAQuota = total valid votes ÷ seats to be filled = minimum votes to win
STV STEP 1Voters mark preferences (1st, 2nd, 3rd…)
STV STEP 2First-preference votes counted; candidates reaching quota declared elected
STV STEP 3Lowest candidate eliminated → their votes transferred to 2nd preference marked on those ballots
STV STEP 4Repeat until the required number are elected (Vidhan Parishad also uses PR by STV)
  • 6 bicameral states (Vidhan Parishad) 📌: Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh
  • Vidhan Sabha = Lower House (directly elected, FPTP); Vidhan Parishad members: elected by Vidhan Sabha members & local bodies + graduates & teachers; Governor nominates some (art, science, social service, literature, co-operatives)
4The Laws Governing Elections 🔥
LawWhat it covers
RP Act, 1950"Rolls & boundaries" — allocation of seats, delimitation of constituencies, preparation & revision of electoral rolls; every citizen 18+ votes without discrimination
RP Act, 1951"Conduct & disputes" — nomination, campaigns, voting procedures, post-election disputes; electoral offences & corrupt practices
Presidential & VP Act, 1952Rules for Presidential & Vice-Presidential elections
  • Trick: 1950 = foundation (rolls + delimitation); 1951 = fighting (conduct + disputes)
  • Corrupt practices (RPA 1951) 📌: ① bribery (gift/offer/promise to vote or withdraw) ② communal appeal (religion, caste, race, community, language) ③ misuse of government personnel (gazetted officers, judges/magistrates, armed forces, police, excise)
5Delimitation Commission
  • Boundaries NOT permanent — they change with population density; without delimitation one MP might represent 5 lakh, another 25 lakh → defeats equal representation
  • Delimitation = determining seats & fixing boundaries for Lok Sabha & Vidhan Sabha — seats-to-population ratio as equal as possible
  • Article 82 mandates it 🔥; 4 commissions so far: 1952, 1963, 1973, 2002 (long gap 1973 → 2002)
6Election Commission of India 🔥 (whole section)
  • Constitution vests in the ECI the "superintendence, direction, and control" of the entire election process 📌 (exact phrase!)
  • ECI = autonomous, permanent, constitutional body; established 25 January 1950 → that date = National Voters' Day; conducts LS, RS, Vidhan Sabha, Vidhan Parishad, President & VP elections; Articles 324–329 📌
  • State machinery: Chief Electoral Officer (CEO) (ECI-appointed civil servant); District Magistrates/SDMs/Tahsildars serve as District Election Officers, Returning Officers, Electoral Registration Officers
FunctionKey points
① Electoral rollEnumerators visit every household → rolls per constituency, polling-station-wise; only those on the roll can vote. SIR = update–verify–correct: adds new 18-year-olds, deletes dead/shifted/duplicate/untraceable; claims & objections settled before final publication
② Schedule & datesWhen 5-year term ends OR legislature dissolved; factors: weather, agricultural cycle, school/university exams, festivals
③ Parties & symbolsOnly ECI-registered parties can contest (independents too); ECI insists on inner-party democracy (periodic organisational elections); classification: national / state / RUPP; symbols to recognised parties; quasi-judicial body in recognition & symbol disputes
④ Free, fair & inclusiveIf fairness fails, public confidence weakens. Inclusion: PwDs, senior citizens, transgender persons, PVTGs; voting from home for 85+ & PwDs with 40% benchmark disability — first nationwide in 2024 General Elections; EVMs with Braille

  • National Voters' Day pledge: vote fearlessly, without influence of religion, race, caste, community, language or inducement
7Political Parties
  • Voters learn about candidates, policies & vision primarily through parties; parties set the agenda (manifestos, rallies, debates via media)
  • Functions 📌: organise public opinion, campaign on issues, offer meaningful choices, enable informed decisions, provide governance/accountability/representation — work at the grassroots; India = multi-party system reflecting diversity
  • Defection 🔥: abandoning the party on whose ticket a member was elected (joining another party or acting independently) — "Aaya Ram, Gaya Ram"; seen as political opportunism (or conscience)
  • Anti-Defection Law 📌: passed 1985, 52nd Constitutional Amendment — leaving party voluntarily or voting against the whip → disqualified from the House; Speaker/Chairman decides; purpose = prevent defection, stabilise governments
National Party (any ONE) 📌State Party (any ONE) 📌
① 6% valid votes in 4+ states (LS or Assembly election) + 4 LS seats① 6% votes in state Assembly election + 2 Assembly seats
② 2% of LS seats from at least 3 states② 6% votes in LS election from the state + 1 LS seat
③ Recognised as State Party in 4+ states③ 3% of Assembly seats (or ≥3 seats, whichever is more) ④ 1 LS seat per 25 seats allotted ⑤ 8% of valid votes polled in the state
  • RUPP: parties without enough votes/seats, or never contested since registration
  • Coalitions ⭐: 1967 = end of single-party era, alliances begin; 1977 Janata Party = first national coalition (backdrop: the Emergency) 📌; since late 1990s: NDA (BJP-led) & UPA (INC-led; dissolved 2023, reconstituted as the INDIA alliance)
8Challenges to Free & Fair Elections
  • Scale: 96.8 crore voters (2024), thousands of stations, hundreds of parties, diverse regions
  • Key challenges: misinformation, fake news, intimidation (+ money power); misinformation manipulates opinion → confusion, even conflict
  • ECI's remedies 📌: RPA 1950 & 1951, MCC, VVPAT, voters' awareness campaigns + constant vigilance + active citizens
9Case Study — Ishani's First Vote 📌 (source-based Q)
STORY (state assembly elections)
Ishani (just turned 18) registered online via ECI portal; went with mother & disabled brother; police-guarded station; wheelchair + volunteers; only 3 officials on duty; forgot Voter ID & Aadhaar — still allowed to vote (other valid documents); saw her VVPAT slip; her 89-year-old grandmother voted from home; wondered how police on duty vote.
Takeaways: ① online registration + PwD inclusion ② other documents can replace Voter ID ③ VVPAT verifies the vote ④ service voters (police/army) use postal ballots — ETPBS. MCC violations shown: wall writings, posters & pamphlets near election day.
Elections = "festival of democracy" — no voter left behind (station even for a single voter). Debate: "One Nation, One Election" — efficiency vs limitations.
10💡 Hinglish Me Samjho — difficult concepts, ek line mein
Elections ka importance: democracy ki soul — Accountability (warna 5 saal baad nikal denge), Representation, Legitimacy (janta ki mandate se sarkar), Equality (PM ki vote = kiraye-daar ki vote).
FPTP vs PR: FPTP = race ka winner (sabse aage, chahe 30% hi ho — Haritbhumi); Majority = "50%+ dikhaao, nahi to top-2 dobara"; PR = pie — jitne % votes, utni % seats (Swarnalok). STV = PR + preference transfer.
Delimitation: classes ko sections mein divide karna taaki har section mein barabar students — warna ek MLA 5 lakh logon ka, doosra 25 lakh ka!
Defection "Aaya Ram Gaya Ram": party X ki ticket se jeeta, phir chupke se party Y mein — isliye 1985 mein 52nd Amendment: party chhodi ya whip ke against vote → disqualify; Speaker faisla karta hai.
Fake news ka kya karna hai: WhatsApp pe "forward karo warna vote cancel" = sab fake! ECI ka jawab: MCC + VVPAT (receipt — EVM galat to nahi?) + awareness. Tumhara jawab: verify karo, aankh band karke forward mat karo!
11🔥 MUST REMEMBER — if nothing else, this!
  • Elections = soul of democracy: ARLE — accountability, representation, legitimacy, equality
  • Direct: LS, Vidhan Sabha, local bodies (every 5 yrs) | Indirect: President, VP, Rajya Sabha
  • FPTP → LS & Vidhan Sabha; PR-STV → RS, President, VP, Vidhan Parishad; FPTP: most votes wins even <50%; PR: seats ∝ votes
  • STV: quota = valid votes ÷ seats; lowest eliminated → transfer to 2nd preference
  • 6 bicameral states: AP, Bihar, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Telangana, UP
  • RPA 1950 = seats, delimitation, rolls, 18+ | RPA 1951 = nomination, campaigns, voting, disputes, corrupt practices
  • Delimitation: Article 82; 4 commissions — 1952, 1963, 1973, 2002
  • ECI: 25 Jan 1950 (National Voters' Day); Articles 324–329; "superintendence, direction & control"
  • Anti-Defection: 1985, 52nd Amendment — leaving party / defying whip → disqualification (Speaker/Chairman decides)
  • Coalitions: 1967 alliance era; 1977 Janata = first national coalition; NDA (BJP) & UPA → INDIA alliance (2023)
  • Challenges → remedies: misinformation, fake news, intimidation → RPA, MCC, VVPAT, awareness campaigns

14📝 How to Use These Points in an Answer
  • Formula: Introduction (1–2 lines — define the system/institution) → Body (3–5 numbered points with keywords + the textbook example) → Conclusion (1 line evaluation). Underline acts, articles & dates.
  • Q: "Explain the First-Past-The-Post system with an example." (3–5m): Intro = FPTP (plurality) chosen by the framers for LS & Vidhan Sabha. Body = ① one vote per candidate in constituency ② MOST votes wins — even <50% of votes polled ③ example: Haritbhumi — A (40%) beats B (35%) & C (25%), forms government ④ contrast: majority system (needs >50%, two rounds) & PR (seats proportional). Conclusion = FPTP is simple with clear winners, though a winner may lack majority support.
  • Q: "Describe the functions of the Election Commission of India." (5m): Intro = ECI (25 Jan 1950; Articles 324–329) autonomous permanent constitutional body with superintendence, direction & control. Body = ① prepares & revises rolls (enumerators, SIR, claims & objections) ② decides schedule (weather, agricultural cycle, exams, festivals) ③ registers parties, allots symbols, quasi-judicial disputes, inner-party democracy ④ free, fair & inclusive — MCC, VVPAT, PwD/85+/transgender/PVTG inclusion, digital apps ⑤ state machinery: CEO → DEOs, ROs, EROs. Conclusion = ECI upholds the world's largest democratic exercise.
  • Q (case study): "From Ishani's first vote — (a) ECI initiatives? (b) which other document? (c) MCC violations?" (5m): ① online registration through ECI portal ② wheelchair + volunteers for her brother (PwD inclusion — Saksham) ③ voting from home for the 89-year-old grandmother (85+ & 40% benchmark PwDs) ④ other officially valid documents (passport, driving licence…) accepted without Voter ID ⑤ VVPAT slip verified her vote; violations = wall writings, posters, pamphlets near polling day; police on duty vote as service voters via ETPBS. Conclusion = "no voter is left behind."
